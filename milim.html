Privacy Policy — Milim

Last updated: 27 August 2026

Milim (“the app”) is an offline personal spending tracker. This policy describes what the app does with your information. The short version is that it does not send any of it anywhere, because it has no way to.

Milim has no servers, no account, no analytics and no internet permission. Every piece of information it holds stays in the app’s private storage on your phone until you delete the app or export it yourself.

What Milim reads

Notifications, only if you switch it on. Milim can use Android’s notification access to read alerts as they appear in your notification shade, which is how a card purchase is recorded without you typing it in.

Three things about this are worth being precise about:

  • Android grants notification access as one switch, so the app sees every notification your phone posts. Milim does not record them. When an alert arrives from a sender you have not marked as a bank, the app keeps only the sender’s name and a short example of the message — with every number removed — so it can offer that sender to you on the setup screen. That means no verification code, card number or amount from an unapproved sender is ever stored. Nothing is filed as spending until you tick that sender.
  • Notifications from chat and social apps — WhatsApp, Messenger, Facebook, Instagram, Telegram and others — are discarded the moment they arrive and are never stored at all.
  • Notification access is optional. If you never grant it, everything else in the app still works and you add spending by pasting a message in, sharing one from your messaging app, or typing it.

You can withdraw it at any time in Android Settings → Notifications → Notification access, or by using “Stop tracking” inside Milim.

Messages you hand over. Text you paste into the app or share into it from another app is read the same way and stored the same way.

What you type. Cash transactions, categories, budgets, assets, debts, gold weights and the prices you enter.

What Milim stores, and where

All of it in the app’s own private directory on your device:

  • Transactions read from bank messages, including the original message text, so a misreading can be corrected later
  • The rules you have taught it about your bank’s message wording
  • Categories, merchants and the category you filed each merchant under
  • Budgets and which budget warnings have already been shown
  • Assets, debts and gold holdings, with the values and prices you entered
  • Your settings, including which senders you chose to track
  • If you set an app lock: a PBKDF2 hash of your PIN, never the PIN itself

Android’s automatic cloud backup is switched off for this app, so none of this is copied to Google Drive. Device-to-device transfer is switched off for the same reason. To move to a new phone, use the app’s own backup, which produces a file you control and can encrypt with a password you choose.

What Milim sends

Nothing. The app requests no internet permission. There is no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, and no third-party SDK that collects anything.

Exchange rates and gold prices are typed in by you, because there is nothing in the app that could fetch them.

The About screen offers links to this policy, to rigprosolutions.com and to an email address. Opening one hands the address to the browser or mail app you already have; Milim itself never opens a connection, which is why it still declares no INTERNET permission.

Sharing

Milim never shares your data with anyone. The only way information leaves your phone is when you deliberately export it — a CSV spreadsheet or a backup file — and then it goes wherever you send it, which is your choice and outside this app.

Permissions, and why each one exists

PermissionWhy
Notification accessOptional. Reads bank alerts so purchases record themselves.
Post notificationsTo warn you when a budget is running low.
Biometric / fingerprintOptional. To unlock the app instead of typing the PIN.
VibrateSo a budget warning can buzz. Added by the notification component.

That is the whole list. Milim does not request permission to read SMS, contacts, location, the camera, or files outside what you explicitly pick — and it does not request internet access, which is what makes the rest of this policy something you can check rather than something you have to believe.

Children

Milim is not directed at children and collects nothing from anyone.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling the app deletes everything it holds. There is no copy anywhere else for us to delete, and no account to close.
